Structuring content around clusters, features and comparisons

Turning keyword and competitor research into a clearer site structure and pages that match what people search for.

My role
On-page SEO and content optimisation
Team
Worked alongside two content writers
Focus
Blog, feature and comparison pages

Context

ContentStudio publishes a large blog alongside feature pages and comparison pages. A blog only builds topical authority if related pages are connected and each page has a clear job. Feature and comparison pages also need to match what buyers actually search for.

What I did

  • Researched before writing. Keyword research and competitor analysis came before new or improved feature pages, so each page targets a defined set of queries.
  • Built comparison pages for competitor-related searches, where buyers are already weighing their options.
  • Optimised existing posts by checking search intent and competing pages, then improving titles, meta descriptions, headings, FAQs and structure so the page answers the query it targets.
  • Organised content into clusters. I set clear parent and child pages, added breadcrumbs and strengthened internal links between related articles and the commercial pages they support.
  • Found gaps by comparing our coverage with competitors' and mapping new keywords to the right page instead of creating overlapping articles.

My part

I handled keyword and competitor research, page optimisation, internal linking and cluster structure. Two content writers produced much of the new content.

Outcome

Over 2025–26, our combined content and SEO work grew organic traffic to the blog by about 100K. This was a team result, shared with the two writers.

Work for ContentStudio, a product of D4 Interactive. Figures come from internal reporting and SEO tools. Dashboards are confidential, but I'm happy to explain how each number was measured.
